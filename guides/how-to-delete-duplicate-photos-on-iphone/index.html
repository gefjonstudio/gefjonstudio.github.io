How to Delete Duplicate Photos on iPhone: 5 Safe Steps

To delete duplicate photos on iPhone, open Photos, go to Albums (or Collections on iOS 26), scroll to Utilities, tap Duplicates, then tap Select, Select All and Merge. That clears exact copies in about a minute. It won't touch near-copies like 6 almost identical shots of the same sunset, and the space doesn't come back until you empty Recently Deleted. Those two gaps are where most people get stuck.

Here are 5 steps in the order I'd do them, with real numbers, so you know how much space to expect.

The quick answer

  • Step 1, 2 minutes: merge the Duplicates album (iOS 16 or later).
  • Step 2, 10 minutes: go through near-copies and bursts by hand, or with a tool that picks the best shot for you.
  • Step 3, 2 minutes: delete old screenshots and the biggest videos.
  • Step 4, 30 seconds: empty Recently Deleted. This is the step that frees the space.
  • Step 5: check iCloud Photos before you delete anything, because deleting on one device deletes everywhere.

Why you have duplicates in the first place

Duplicates aren't always a mistake you made. The usual sources:

  • Saving the same photo twice from Messages, WhatsApp or a download.
  • Edits and exports that leave the original and a copy.
  • Burst-style tapping. Hold-tapping the shutter at a birthday gives you 8 frames where 1 would do.
  • Restoring from a backup or importing from a camera into a library that already had the photos.
  • Screenshots of photos you already own.

Worked example: a phone photo usually takes up roughly 2 to 4 MB, depending on the camera and format. Say you have 1,200 duplicates and near-copies. At about 3 MB each, that's roughly 3.6 GB, or about the same as a 1-hour 4K video. Not life-changing for a 256 GB phone, but it's a real chunk when you're stuck on a 64 GB one with the red "storage full" bar.

1. Merge the built-in Duplicates album

Apple added this in iOS 16, so check your version first (Settings, General, About). If you're on iOS 16 or newer:

  1. Open Photos.
  2. Tap Albums (on iOS 26, tap Collections).
  3. Scroll down to Utilities and tap Duplicates.
  4. Tap Select, then Select All, then Merge, and confirm.

Apple's own wording is that merging combines the highest quality version and the relevant data (captions, keywords, favorites) and keeps one copy in your library. The rest go to Recently Deleted.

Two catches:

  • If the album isn't there, you don't have any exact duplicates it can find, or the phone is still analysing your library. Plug it in, leave it on Wi-Fi overnight and check again.
  • It handles duplicates, not similar photos. Two portrait shots taken a second apart are different files, so Photos leaves them alone.

Worked example: you merge 84 sets. Each set was two copies at about 3 MB, so you get back about 252 MB. That's useful, but you've only done the easy part.

2. Deal with the near-copies

This is where the real space is, and no built-in button does it for you. You have three ways:

  • By hand, month by month. Open Photos, tap Library, then All, and scroll through a single month. Look for runs of 3 or more shots of the same scene. Keep the sharpest one, with eyes open. Delete the rest.
  • Use the Bursts album. Under Utilities you'll often find Bursts. Pick the shots you like and Photos discards the rest.

Worked example: you have 5 shots of a dog at the park. Four are slightly blurry, one is sharp. Deleting 4 at about 3 MB each saves 12 MB. Do that for 100 scenes and you've freed about 1.2 GB, with the best picture of each scene still there.

A rule worth following: never delete from a preselected list without looking at it once. Even good tools can rate a blurry shot of your kid's first steps below a sharp shot of a wall.

3. Clear the screenshots and the heavy videos

Photos often aren't what fills a phone. Videos and screenshots can take far more room, so do this while you're already in a cleaning mood.

  • Screenshots: open Albums, scroll to Media Types and tap Screenshots. Tap Select and delete the ones you don't need. Receipts and boarding passes you've already used are safe to go.
  • Videos: tap Videos in the same list and scan for the longest clips. They're usually the biggest.

Apple publishes approximate sizes for its camera settings. As a rough guide, 1 minute of 4K at 60 fps takes up around 400 MB when using the high efficiency format, while 1 minute of 1080p at 30 fps takes up around 60 MB.

Worked example: you find three 4K 60 fps clips of 2 minutes each from a holiday. That's roughly 2.4 GB for 6 minutes of footage. If you only need to keep 30 seconds of each, trimming them or deleting the failed takes saves more space than deleting 800 photos.

4. Empty Recently Deleted (this is the step people miss)

When you delete a photo on iPhone, it doesn't disappear. It goes to Recently Deleted and stays there for up to 30 days. Storage doesn't come back until it's gone from there too.

  1. Open Albums and scroll to Utilities, then tap Recently Deleted.
  2. Unlock it with Face ID if asked.
  3. Tap Select, then Delete All, and confirm.

Worked example: you merge duplicates and delete screenshots and your Settings, General, iPhone Storage screen still shows the same number. You open Recently Deleted and find 3.1 GB waiting. Delete All, wait a few seconds and the storage bar drops.

This is final, so take 10 seconds to scan it first. If you aren't sure about a photo, tap it and choose Recover instead.

5. Check iCloud Photos before you delete

If iCloud Photos is on (Settings, your name, iCloud, Photos), your library is one library across every device. Delete a photo on your iPhone and it's deleted on your iPad and the web too. Apple's guidance is that deleted photos sit in Recently Deleted on all of them for 30 days.

That has two consequences:

  • Deleting duplicates doesn't free your iCloud plan on its own until they leave Recently Deleted. If your goal is getting under the free 5 GB, empty it.
  • Optimize iPhone Storage keeps small versions on the phone and full-size originals in iCloud. With that on, each photo takes up less room on the device than its full size, so your phone-side saving per photo will be smaller than the numbers above. Settings, General, iPhone Storage shows the real picture.

If you don't use iCloud Photos, deleting is local and you should take a backup first (a Mac, or a plain iCloud Backup).

Mistakes that cost people photos

  • Merging without looking when two photos look the same but one is an edited version you wanted. Merge keeps the "best", which isn't always the one you'd pick.
  • Deleting a whole month in one go because the first 20 photos looked like junk. Work in small batches.
  • Emptying Recently Deleted too early. Wait a day or two if you did a huge clean in one go.

FAQ

How do I delete duplicate photos on iPhone all at once?

On iOS 16 or newer, open Photos, go to Albums or Collections, then Utilities, then Duplicates. Tap Select, Select All and Merge. That handles exact duplicates only. Similar shots need a manual pass or a cleaning tool.

Why don't I see a Duplicates album on my iPhone?

It only appears on iOS 16 or later, and only when Photos has found exact duplicates. If your phone is up to date and it's missing, leave the phone charging on Wi-Fi so Photos can finish analysing the library, then look again in a day.

Does deleting duplicate photos free up space right away?

No. Deleted photos move to Recently Deleted for up to 30 days. Open that album, tap Select, then Delete All to free the space straight away.

Will deleting duplicates on my iPhone delete them from iCloud?

Yes, if iCloud Photos is on. It keeps all your devices in sync, so a deletion on one shows up on the rest.

Is it safe to use a duplicate photo cleaner app?

It can be, if it runs on your phone, explains what it will delete and lets you review before anything goes. Avoid apps that upload your library, ask you to sign up, or lock deleting behind a weekly fee.
