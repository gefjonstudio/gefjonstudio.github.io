To sign a document on iPhone, open the PDF in the Files app, tap the Markup button (the pen tip in a circle, 1 tap), tap the plus sign, choose Add Signature, sign with your finger, tap Done, drag the signature onto the line and tap Done again. Your iPhone saves that signature, so the next document takes 3 taps. No printer, no scanner, no account.

That works for a clean PDF. It stops working when the file is a Word document, a photo of a form, a paper page on your desk, or a 12-page lease that needs your initials on every page. This guide covers the 5 situations you'll actually meet, what to do in each, and the small mistakes that get a signed document sent back.

Quick answer: which method for which file

  1. A PDF in Files, Mail or Safari: use Markup. Free, built in, about 30 seconds.
  2. A screenshot or photo of a form: use Markup in Photos. Fine for informal forms, but the result is an image, not a PDF.
  3. A Microsoft Word, Apple Pages or Excel file: convert it to PDF first, then use Markup. Or use an app that opens it directly.
  4. A paper page: scan it with the Notes app, then sign the scan.
  5. A long document with initials on every page, or a second person signing in the room: use a signing app built for it.

1. Sign a PDF with Markup (the built-in way)

Apple documents this method on its own support page, and for a 1-off PDF it's tough to beat.

  1. Tap the PDF in the Files app, Apple Mail or Safari so it opens in the preview.
  2. Tap the Markup button, the pen tip inside a circle.
  3. Tap the + button, then Add Signature.
  4. Sign with your finger. If you don't like it, tap Clear and try again.
  5. Tap Done, then drag the signature to the line. Drag any dot on its outline to resize it.
  6. Tap Done, then share or save the file.

2 details save time. First, you can save several signatures, such as your full name and your initials, under Add or Remove Signature. Second, the signature is placed as a movable object until you save, so you can still nudge it before you send.

Worked example. A landlord emails you a 1-page lease renewal as a PDF. Open it from Apple Mail on iOS 17, tap Markup, Add Signature, drag it to the "Tenant" line on page 1, tap Done, then reply with the file attached. Total time: about 40 seconds the first time, 20 seconds after that.

2. Sign a screenshot or photo of a form

Take a screenshot of the form (side button plus Volume Up), tap the preview in the corner, then tap the Markup button and use the same + > Add Signature steps. You can also do this to any photo in the Photos app by tapping Edit, then the Markup button.

Here's where it falls short:

  • The result is an image file (a PNG or JPEG), not a PDF. Many offices, banks and property managers want a PDF and will bounce an image.
  • A screenshot only captures what's on screen, so a 3-page document means 3 screenshots to stitch together.

Worked example. A soccer coach texts a photo of a 1-page waiver at 7 pm on Friday. You screenshot it, sign it, and send it straight back in the same thread. That's fine. A bank asking for a 6-page signed loan form wants a PDF, so don't send it a photo.

3. Sign a Word, Pages or Excel file

This is where most people get stuck. Files and Mail will happily preview a .docx, but the preview doesn't offer a signature tool. You have 3 options:

  • Export to PDF first. In Apple Pages or Microsoft Word, tap Share (or the three dots), choose Export, pick PDF, then sign the PDF with Markup. This is free and takes about a minute, but it only works if the app that made the file is installed.
  • Ask the sender for a PDF. Often the quickest. A contractor who sends a quote as a Word file can usually send a PDF in one reply.
  • Use an app that opens the file type directly. SignaturePro opens Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Pages, Numbers and Keynote files, plus HEIC photos, and turns them into a PDF on your phone before you sign.

Worked example. A client sends a 4-page contract as a .docx file, due back by 5 pm. You open it in Apple Pages, Share, Export, PDF. Then Markup, sign on page 4. Result: 1 signed 4-page PDF to send back, in about 2 minutes.

4. Sign a paper document

If the document is on paper and you can't print or scan, use the Notes app.

  1. Open Apple Notes and start a new note.
  2. Tap the camera button, then Scan Documents.
  3. Hold the phone over the page. Auto-capture takes the picture when it sees the edges.
  4. Tap Save, then tap the scan to open it.
  5. Tap the Markup button, then + > Add Signature.

For a signature only (to drop into other files), you can also sign a blank sheet in ink, photograph it, and use a signing app that lifts the ink off the paper and reuses it.

Worked example. A school hands you a 2-page form on paper at 3 pm pickup. You scan both pages in about 20 seconds, sign page 2, and email it from the car. Check the scan for shadows and cut-off edges before you send. A page that reads badly gets rejected as quickly as an unsigned one.

5. Sign a long document or several signers

Markup is made for 1 signature on 1 page, and it gets tedious when:

  • A lease or offer letter has 11 pages that each need your initials.
  • You need to add a date, a checkbox and a typed name as well as a signature.
  • A second person in the room has to sign on your phone.
  • You want proof of who signed and when.

How long each method takes

Here are rough timings from a typical 1-page and 12-page job, on iOS 17 or later:

  • Markup on a 1-page PDF: about 40 seconds the first time, 20 seconds after that.
  • Screenshot and sign: about 25 seconds, plus 10 seconds per extra page.
  • Pages or Word to PDF, then Markup: about 2 minutes for a 4-page file.
  • Scan in Notes, then sign: about 1 minute for 2 pages.
  • 12-page lease with initials on 11 pages: about 11 drags in Markup, or 1 placement plus Copy to All Pages in a signing app.

These are my own estimates, not lab results.

Make it look right: 6 quick checks

  1. Zoom before you sign. Put the signature on the line, not over the words next to it. A signature that covers a printed name looks careless.
  2. Size it to a real signature. Roughly the height of a handwritten line is right. Shrink or grow it with the corner dots.
  3. Match your usual signature. A bank comparing it to your ID will notice a neat italic version.
  4. Initial every page that asks. Missing initials is a common reason a 12-page lease comes back to you.
  5. Flatten before sending. A flattened PDF has the signature baked in, so it can't be dragged off. If you share from a tool that leaves it as a movable object, the receiver might see it in a different spot.
  6. Keep the original. Save the signed copy as "Lease-signed.pdf" and keep the unsigned file.

Is a signature drawn on an iPhone legal?

For most everyday agreements in the US, yes. The federal ESIGN Act says a signature can't be denied legal effect only because it's electronic, and most states have a matching law. A drawn signature, a typed name and a clicked "I agree" can all count, as long as you intended to sign.

There are exceptions. ESIGN does not cover wills and testamentary trusts, many family law matters such as adoption and divorce, and court documents and orders. Some documents also need witnesses or a notary, which an iPhone signature can't replace. Rules differ by state and country, so for a mortgage, a court filing or anything needing a notary, ask the organisation what it accepts first. This isn't legal advice.

Frequently asked questions

Can I sign a PDF on iPhone for free?

Yes. Markup in Files, Mail, Notes and Photos is built into iOS and costs nothing. It works well for single PDFs. You only need an app when the file isn't a PDF, when you need initials on many pages, or when you want a record of who signed.

Can I sign a Word document on iPhone?

Not directly in Files, no. Export it to PDF from Word or Pages, then sign the PDF with Markup. An app that opens Word files can do the conversion and signing in one go.

Why does my signature look shaky?

Finger signatures on a 6-inch screen are rough, and we've all done it. Sign slowly over 3 to 4 seconds, use the whole pad, and tap Clear until it looks right. In some apps you can type your name in a handwriting font or photograph a signature you signed on paper.

Does the other person see my signature as a picture or as part of the document?

If you export a flattened PDF, it's part of the page. If you screenshot and sign, it's part of an image. Either way they see it. Flattening just stops anyone moving it later.
