How to Invoice a Client for Services: Step by Step, With 6 Worked Examples

To invoice a client for services, put eight things on one page: your details, the client's details, a unique invoice number, the issue date, a due date, itemised services with prices, the total, and how to pay you. Then send it the same day the work is done, in the format your client actually opens.

That's the short version. The hard part is the maths and the wording when a job isn't simple: a deposit was paid, you bought materials, the work ran in stages, or the client is late. This guide covers the basics first, then walks through six real situations with numbers you can copy.

Quick answer

  1. Agree the price and payment terms before you start, in writing.
  2. Give every invoice its own number, in order.
  3. List each service on its own line with a quantity and a price.
  4. Show the subtotal, any tax or discount, anything already paid, and the amount due.
  5. Put a due date on it, not just "net 30".
  6. Say exactly how to pay, ideally with a link or QR code that has the amount filled in.
  7. Send it the day the work is finished, and note when you sent it.
  8. Follow up before the due date, not only after.

The 8 things every service invoice needs

  • Your details. Business or personal name, address, phone, email. Add a licence or tax number if your trade or country needs one.
  • The client's details. Use the name of whoever pays the bill, which is not always the person you worked with. Larger clients often have an accounts payable contact.
  • An invoice number. Make it unique and sequential, such as 2026-001, 2026-002. Your accountant and your client both need to refer to it later.
  • Issue date. The day you send it.
  • Due date. Write the actual date. "Net 14" issued on November 3 means due November 17. "Net 30" issued the same day means due December 3.
  • Line items. One line per service: description, quantity (or hours), rate, line total.
  • Totals. Subtotal, tax, discounts, deposits already paid, and a bold "Amount due".
  • Payment instructions. Bank transfer details, PayPal, Venmo, Zelle, Cash App, or a card link. Pick two or three, not eight.

Tax rules differ by country, state and type of work. Some places require a tax number on the invoice or want tax shown on a separate line. If you aren't sure, ask an accountant or your local tax office once, then set it up in your template and forget it.

Six worked examples

1. Hourly work

You're a freelance editor. You logged 12.5 hours at $80 an hour.

  • Line item: "Copy editing, Q3 report, 12.5 hrs × $80.00" = $1,000.00
  • Tax: none (check yours)
  • Amount due: $1,000.00
  • Terms: net 14, issued November 3, due November 17

Tip: write the hours and rate on the line, not just the total. Clients rarely argue with a number they can multiply themselves.

2. Flat fee with a deposit

A painter quotes $2,400 to repaint a living room and hall, and asks for 50% up front.

  • Deposit invoice before the job: $1,200.00
  • Final invoice after the job:
  • Interior repaint (quoted): $2,400.00
  • Less deposit paid November 3: −$1,200.00
  • Amount due: $1,200.00

The mistake to avoid is sending the final invoice for only $1,200 with no mention of the deposit. Always show the full price, then subtract what's been paid. That way the paperwork adds up on its own if anyone checks it a year later.

3. Labour plus materials plus tax

A plumber replaces a kitchen tap. Sales tax is 8% and, in this example, applies to materials only. (Many places tax parts and not labour. Some tax both. Check your own rules.)

  • Labour: 6 hrs × $65.00 = $390.00
  • Tap: $84.50
  • Fittings and sealant: $23.50
  • Materials subtotal: $108.00
  • Tax on materials, 8%: $8.64
  • Amount due: $506.64

Show materials at cost or with your markup, but be consistent. If you add a markup, say so in your quote first.

4. A job paid in stages

A $9,000 bathroom refit is billed 30% / 40% / 30%.

  • Invoice 1, on starting: $2,700.00
  • Invoice 2, when the tiling is done: $3,600.00
  • Invoice 3, on completion: $2,700.00

Number them 2026-014-A, B and C, or note "Stage 2 of 3" on each line, so nobody pays the same stage twice. Each invoice should list the stage and the running total: "Paid to date: $2,700.00. Remaining after this invoice: $2,700.00."

5. A monthly retainer with extra hours

A designer gets $1,500 a month for up to 10 hours. In October she used 13.5 hours, and extra hours are $150.

  • Overage: 3.5 hrs × $150 = $525.00
  • November retainer: $1,500.00
  • Invoice dated November 1: $2,025.00

Say on the line what the overage was for, with dates. "October extra hours: 3.5" is much more likely to be paid without a question than a lump sum.

6. A late payment

Your $506.64 invoice from example 3 is now seven days overdue. Send a short message that restates the facts:

Hi Sam, a quick reminder that invoice 2026-021 for $506.64 was due on November 17. You can pay by the QR code on the invoice or by Zelle. Let me know if anything is unclear.

If you want to charge a late fee, put the rate on the invoice and in your original agreement. As an example, 1.5% a month on $506.64 is $7.60. Many places cap late fees or require them to be agreed beforehand, so check before adding one to an invoice you've already sent.

Make it easy to pay

The most common reason an invoice sits unpaid is friction. The client has to find your bank details, copy them, and type the amount. Each step is a chance to put it off until tomorrow.

Three habits that help:

  • Put the amount in the payment link. A PayPal or Venmo link with the amount pre-filled saves the client a step. A QR code on the invoice does the same thing for someone looking at it on a laptop with their phone in hand. Invoice Once builds that scan-to-pay code for you from your PayPal, Venmo or Cash App details.
  • Offer a way that suits the client. A homeowner might prefer Zelle or Cash App. A company will probably want a bank transfer.
  • Send the invoice the same day. Work finished on Friday and invoiced the next Thursday trains clients to pay late.

How to send it

PDF by email is the standard. Use a subject line that can be searched later: "Invoice 2026-021 for kitchen tap, due Nov 17". In the message, restate the amount and due date in one sentence.

Some clients, especially homeowners, would rather get it by text. A PDF doesn't always arrive through SMS, so send page images or a link, and put the amount and how to pay in the message itself.

Don't rely on a chat message alone. You want a record with a date on it.

Follow up on a schedule

A simple rhythm that works for most small jobs:

  • 3 days before the due date: friendly heads-up with the amount and payment link.
  • On the due date: short note that it's due today.
  • 7 days late: restate the amount and date, attach the invoice again.
  • 14 days late: call or message directly, and ask when you can expect payment.

Keep a note of each contact. If it ever becomes a dispute, a dated trail of polite messages is worth far more than a memory.

Common mistakes

  • Vague descriptions. "Services rendered" invites questions. "Replaced kitchen tap, 6 hrs" doesn't.
  • No due date. Without one, the client sets their own.
  • Duplicate numbers. Two invoices with the same number confuse your records and your client's.
  • Forgetting the deposit. Show it as a line, so the balance is obviously correct.
  • Sending to the wrong person. Check who handles payments before you hit send.
  • Waiting for the end of the month. Invoice when the work is done.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a special invoice template?

No. Any clean document with the eight items above works. A reusable template saves time and keeps your numbering and layout consistent, which makes you look organised and makes your records easier to keep.

Should I invoice before or after the work?

Small jobs are usually invoiced after. For larger jobs, ask for a deposit first, as in example 2, and bill in stages, as in example 4. It protects your cash flow and shows the client you're serious.

What is the difference between an estimate and an invoice?

An estimate (or quote) is the price you offer before the work. An invoice is the request for payment after, or during, the work. A good flow is: estimate, client approves, deposit invoice, work, final invoice with the deposit subtracted.

How soon should a client pay?

It's your call, but 7 to 14 days is common for small jobs and 30 days for larger companies. Whatever you choose, write the actual date on the invoice.

Can I invoice from my phone?
